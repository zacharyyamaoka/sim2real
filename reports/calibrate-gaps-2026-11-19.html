FF-2 feedforward rounds and the committed ladder landed

lane L8 · ruler tests/test_feedforward_rounds.py, plus the toy fix cda06cd

  • FeedforwardBench iterates on A2's rounds. Round 1 is the slow band. Each later round collects the empty cells, then the cells whose held-out residual is over tolerance, worst first, as fresh runs carrying the last fit's feedforward.
  • It converges when gravity moves less than delta_nm between rounds, the worst held-out cell is within tolerance and no cell is empty. It stops "no improvement" only when it is over tolerance with every cell collected. When every cell fits but ΔG hasn't been read yet, it re-collects the worst cells instead of stopping unconverged.
  • Bench row rules for a board's PD: a moving row is |qd| ≥ 0.1 rad/s (5.7°/s), and the two legs pair within 0.05 rad (2.9°).
  • Review fix: on a dataset grown by session, X and X@session are one run in one cell, so a refit finds its round fits and holds out both recordings together.
  • The committed hidden-truth ladder: see the feedforward section.

Feedforward calibration: where it stands

What the arm runs today. A lot of feedforward, but none of it came from a routine:

  • gravity from measured masses on J2/C1 and the wrist (PID tuna's 15c file);
  • J1's sine gravity law (two laws are live; they agree within 0.19 N·m);
  • Coulomb friction while moving: the J2/C1 0.70 / 0.78 N·m is a bench constant, never refit on bam_fb_v2;
  • an inertial term from the trajectory, using the CAD masses;
  • the gated hold pushes on J2/C1;
  • two five-bar compensations; the lumped one is about 0.65 N·m off on C1 at a family −1 wrist.

Each came from one-off PID tuna scripts over runs harvested from picks, checked on held-out runs and installed by a pin move. The fits are good, but the process doesn't repeat and has no stop rule.

What sim2real now has: arm.feedforward_bench, your collect → fit → iterate.

  1. Collect: paired out-and-back moves from the trajectory library, at two speeds, over a workspace pose graph. Each command already carries the current feedforward.
  2. Fit: gravity, plus Coulomb and viscous friction per drive. The way back cancels the friction, and holds never count.
  3. Check: each fit is tested on held-out cells.
  4. Iterate: each new round collects only what is missing or still off, with the last fit's feedforward. It stops when the fit stops changing, stops improving, or hits its cap.

Bench is this routine, submitted through rackq once the twin passes. Deployed is the same fit over the production runs the twin now holds (gap D), with PID tuna's learned feedforward as a drift canary.
