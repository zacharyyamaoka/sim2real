How it rolls up

  • The verdict is the first "no": "stops at Data" means Floor passed and Data did not; the later answers are still computed and shown beside it.
  • A parent passes a question only if it answers yes at its own output (an arm at its tool pose, a deployment at its picks) and every child passes it (CalibrationResult.verdicts()). So the deployment's line is the answer for the whole rig, and a joint that is twinned on its own cannot make an arm twinned whose coupled moves are not.
  • The numbers roll up softly: medians of the children, the worst child's twin ratio, the tool in mm.

The rules behind each gate

  • Floor, from same-input repeats only (metrics.series.repeat_floor). Repeats are grouped per run kind and controller gains (T1: like for like) and compared tick for tick when the run carries its plan tick: on the host's wall clock, ~1 ms of jitter put identical five-bar tapes 1.6–3.1° apart. The floor check: with each real run = truth + independent noise, floor² = 2σ² and gap² = bias² + σ², so a twin cannot sit below 1/√2 ≈ 0.71× its floor; one that does fails Floor (its "repeats" were not repeats), it does not pass Twin.
  • Twin on the floor's own run kind: the wrist's twin is judged on its repeat campaign's tape (cycle-s4, cycle14-a), the five-bar's on the five repeats of the 0.05 m/s gravity-feed-forward rung, replayed on the after twin.
  • Passive "N/A with a measured reason" (proposed, Zach to confirm): a joint that cannot move unpowered answers N/A instead of failing Floor forever. CAN 15 and 16 carry at most 0.012 N·m of gravity against 0.140 and 0.186 N·m of breakaway; the five-bar's J2 and C1 rest at the closure fold, where gravity cannot move them, and larger releases fall 40–80° at up to 1,300°/s.
  • Data thresholds (proposed, coordinator 2026-11-11, Zach to confirm): per joint ≥ 40 % of reachable (angle, speed, acceleration) cells; arm: cube ≥ 90 %, workspace ≥ 50 %, facing (wrist) or heading (five-bar) ≥ 90 %. The joint's reachable cells come from its committed description; for these joints every cell of the box counts as reachable, so it is the visited share of the full box.
  • Routines means recovering the hidden truth, in sim, within tolerance, under noise: the oracle's rule (sysid/acceptance.json): every hidden value within its tolerance at 1× sensor noise, and the nominal fails its own tolerance so "return the description unchanged" fails; the noise ladder (sysid/noise_ladder.json): 0×, 1×, 3×, 10×, bound min(k × tol, max(tol, |nominal − true|)).
  • Job: the tool gate is 1 mm at a stop (Zach, 2026-11-11: "we certainly need final tool error sub 1mm", was 5 mm), measured on settled holds: the five-bar's 16 three-second mass-ID holds, not the raster's turnarounds (median 0.06 s: tracking, not settled). A joint's share of it is 1 mm ÷ (lever × √joints), an RSS split (proposed); tools/tool_levers.py measures each rig's lever.

Still sized for 5 mm in code (to re-derive at 1 mm; not changed here):

  • cards/domain_v3.json: arm.tool_error_at_stop.gate, pick.grasp_pose_error.gate and conveyor.object_position.gate are ≤ 5 mm.
  • cards/servo_gates.py: STOP_ALLOCATION_MRAD and T4's rule text allocate "the 5 mm tool gate" (about 3 mm RSS inside 5 mm).
  • sysid/acceptance.json: tolerances are set for "a 5 mm tool budget"; the ur5e examples' held-out tool is 5 mm.

  • The gate order: target vs sim first, then sim vs real, then target vs real. Target vs sim is the controller alone (does the controller do the job in a sim that is right by construction?); sim vs real is the twin (held-out, ≤ 1.5× the same-input repeat floor on the tick clock, the tree's Twin answer); target vs real is the job. A later gate is read only after the earlier ones: a real tool error cannot be blamed on the twin until the controller passes in sim.

2 · The curriculum

How to read it. The verdict reads the five questions in check order (Floor first: a wrong floor makes every later number meaningless). The ladder is climbed in work order: Routines first, because it needs no rig time (prove a routine on a hidden truth in sim before trusting it on hardware), then the rig rungs from cheapest to dearest. A rung passes exactly when its question passes, so the two orders read the same answers; "next rung" and "stops at" can name different questions only when more than one is open. Today they agree on every node. sim2real.tracking.curriculum holds the ladders; each W&B run carries its node's rungs.

  2. Then S4. The after twin is 3.69× (J2) and 2.36× (C1) its floor on the floor's own tape; the gate is 1.5×.
  3. Then the tool gate (A5, T5). The tool settles 4.3 mm from its target by encoders + FK against 1 mm. C1 is the bigger term: it settles 0.637° from its target, 4.4× its 0.144° share (J2: 2.7×).

3 · Tracking it in W&B: three proposals

Your question: "Should we have one weights and biases project per generic type? This could be a lot of projects. But I guess for each one it's a bit unique, no? OK, having it in one location could work, if we can filter that data to show it for particular types of models or something…"

What the question assumes:

  • "A lot of projects" is the cheap part of the cost. The dear part is that per-type projects split one rig's tree: the wrist's deployment, arm, three joints and holder would land in four projects. "A parent passes only if every child does" would then be visible nowhere, and a W&B workspace cannot hold runs from two projects (only a report can).
  • "Each one is a bit unique": less than it looks. Every node answers the same five questions with the same words; that is the point of proposal C. What is unique per type is its Job metric and unit (px, mm, °, %) and its coverage axes: config and summary keys, data rather than structure.
  • What a W&B run is. Not a rig run (the wrist calibration has 400+); those stay in the archive, on the result page and in Rerun (the house rule: "we own our data … W&B only ever gets a copy"). A W&B run is one node's evaluation at one twin iteration, so W&B tracks the verdicts and their numbers over time.

Criteria, weighted for now:

1 One place, filterable at a glance 25 your worry, and "at a glance I can look at any level, and navigate that with the tree"

A · One project per generic type ("Integrated Servo", "Gearbox", "Camera", "Conveyor", "Rigid Body Chain", … up to 17)

  • For: one schema per project and a clean auto-made workspace; one type compares across rigs natively. It mirrors today's split of the pick-place ladder into "Grasp Detection" and "Traj Gen".
  • Against: a rig's tree is spread over 4–6 projects and its roll-up is visible nowhere; a deployment view needs a cross-project report; every new type is a new project with its own views.
  • Scores: one place 1 · like with like 3 (across rigs 5, within a rig 1) · tree 1 · growth 2 · honest numbers 5 · house 4 · sharing 2.

B · One project, Sim2Real, every run configured and filtered (recommended on these criteria and built first; superseded as the home by C per rig, below)

  • For: filter type = integrated_servo and get every servo on every rig; deployment = fivebar and get its whole tree; group by either; one overview over everything. A new type or deployment is a new config value; its view and report are generated from the runs present (build_views).
  • Against: one key namespace, so type-specific keys sit sparse beside each other. Kept honest by units in every key name (_deg, _mm, _pct) and by per-type views that chart only that type's keys.
  • Scores: one place 5 · like with like 5 · tree 5 · growth 4 · honest numbers 3 · house 4 · sharing 3.

C · One project per deployment ("UR Wrist Test Rig", "Five-bar Test Rig", "Conveyor", …), types as config

  • For: it mirrors result.display(): one tree per project; sharing one customer's cell is clean.
  • Against: a type across rigs needs a cross-project report; deployments are the fastest-growing count; the house's own deployment mirror (bam_ws bam_deployments, project bam-deployments) is one project grouped by deployment, not one per deployment.
  • Scores: one place 3 · like with like 2 · tree 5 · growth 2 · honest numbers 3 · house 3 · sharing 5.

My recommendation on these criteria was B (89), and it was built first. What is live is C, per rig, with B's config on every run, because you set the direction tonight (relayed by the W1 sprint): bottom-up per sub-assembly, each rig introspected through W&B and Rerun, in projects named "Sim2Real · five-bar rig" and "Sim2Real · wrist rig" on the house black-box template (bam_ws bam_trackio.bbo: one W&B project per black-box family, its versions on leaderboards). Your call outranks my scoring, and the criteria above did not weigh what it adds:

  • One leaderboard per sub-assembly. A rig is the black box being optimised; its twin iterations and controller configurations are its versions, ranked on the same environment. In one shared project those leaderboards would sit side by side with no owner.

  • What C costs against B, and how it is paid: a workspace cannot filter across rigs, so "every servo on every rig" is a report, not a filter: the cross-rig report in Sim2Real takes one runset per rig, each filtered type = integrated_servo, and charts the same-named gates side by side. Every run carries type, level, deployment and joint, so any new cross-rig report is one filter.

  • Decision record: docs/decision-wandb-one-project-2026-11-11.md, amended: B is superseded as the home by C per rig; B's runs stay in Sim2Real (nothing is deleted) and wandb_log still logs that layout.

  • Reversible either way: the per-rig projects are rigs.RIGS[...].project, the one-project layout is wandb_log.PROJECT; both are written from the same records, so going back to B is one command and no data moves.

  • Steelman for A: you split the pick-place ladder into two projects today because "we are optimizing them separately". That split is between two products with a contract between them and two scoreboards; sim2real has one scoreboard at every level, and splitting it by type would break the roll-up the scoreboard is made of.

  • What would change my mind: a customer who may see only their own cell (C for customer cells); one type's media (camera frames, hyperspectral cubes) swamping the shared workspace even with views (that type's media in its own project, its verdict still here); W&B filters slowing down at scale (untested past a few hundred runs).
