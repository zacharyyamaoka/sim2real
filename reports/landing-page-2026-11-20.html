Bench today, deployed next: what is built and what is designed

The lifetime section answers Zach's question, what does a routine once deployed look like? Once a robot is at work, calibration is mostly watching the fit: monitor → trigger → refit a challenger → shadow-compare → keep if it wins → new record. The landing marks the whole loop designed, not built and links its design, docs/DEPLOYED.md.

Where each routine runs comes from the registry: 24 bench, 7 deployed, 32 that run anywhere, out of 63 routines over 12 part types.

There today

  • Live re-identification while the joint works; its page shows its grades, biased against a Stribeck truth.
  • A gap check every 12 h with a wear forecast. In a sim study on the pendulum's hidden world the fidelity ratio warned at 204 h and the gap check alone at 300 h; the hidden wear crossed its allowance at 292.4 h. At 168 h the forecast named 298.4 h, with plan-by 280.4 h.
  • Camera pose refit from belt tags. In a sim study of a two-arm cell, a camera knocked at 300 h was flagged at 300 h.
  • Cell tags bundle adjustment.
  • Budget(settings=("deployed",)) plans the routines that run on a robot at work plus those that run anywhere, and calibrate(data=…) refits on runs the twin already holds.
  • Each calibration writes a ledger with a W&B copy, and preflight() checks a fit before it ships.
  • No robot has a real deployed record yet.

Designed, not built

  • production: sim2real.calibration.production(): pick sessions in, CalibrationData out, the newest sessions held out.
  • monitor: fitted.monitor(): fidelity, parameter drift with its forecast, performance, coverage, and what is due.
  • recalibrate: fitted.recalibrate(): a challenger refit on recent sessions, kept only if it wins on the same held-out runs.
  • valid_until: a validity window on the record.
  • save_description: fitted.save_description(): the description is written only when you say so.

Build order in docs/DEPLOYED.md: production, then monitor, recalibrate, a validity window, save_description. Decided there: refit from the priors over a window of sessions, never install a challenger by itself, write the description only when asked, PD and feed-forward only.

tests/test_landing_page.py searched the raw landing.json for a serial (eight upper-case hex characters, or ten decimal digits in a row) without first blanking the hash fields, as export_landing.py does.
