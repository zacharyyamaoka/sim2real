Recovery is the worst |error| ÷ tolerance, and ≤ 1 passes. aruco.caliper_size is not in this plan because the bundle already covers print scale: select() picks a minimal cover. You can bring it back with CalibrationConfig(include=("aruco.caliper_size",)).

CalibrationConfig: steering the whole run

import sim2real
import sim2real.calibration as cal

twin = sim2real.examples.ur_wrist_test_rig()
config = cal.CalibrationConfig(
    known={"can14.backlash_rad": 0.00349},          # fixed: every routine sees it, a fit never overwrites it
    exclude=("servo.command_delay_scan",),          # kept out of the plan
    level="3x",                                     # rank alternative routines by their 3x recovery
    budget=cal.Budget(settings=("bench",), max_wall_time_s=3600),
    seed=7,
)
cal.plan(twin, config)            # what would run, in what order, without running anything
fitted = twin.calibrate(on="sim", config=config)
fitted.plan_, fitted.record_      # the plan that ran, and the sim2real.calibration/1 record

Camera: exposure and FPS tuned for ArUco accuracy

camera.exposure_for_aruco is a tune routine. It runs after camera.exposure_lock, requires the marker ids, and refines exposure and gain. Its objective is true corner accuracy, not mean grey and not markers read. The hidden-truth renderer is the test's own: photons, shot and read noise, 8-bit clipping, and motion blur. The tuner sees only captured frames. Values are true mean corner error in px at 1x; the house rule is today's bam_camera_calibration markers-read rule.

The binding scene is moving dim, where the tuner picks 30 fps and the oracle's best is the same exposure at 6 fps. That gap comes from how the oracle samples frames, and the tuner cannot measure it. In the dark it takes 133 ms at 6 fps, which matches the rack's own D435 still look of 6 fps.
