Conveyor belt calibration, proven in sim

2026-11-11 · sim2real branch claude/conveyor-type (not merged; waits on the camera-types lane) · Zach's brief: Daily Note Nov 11, "# Conveyor belt calibration" · research note

Nine belt calibration routines recover a hidden truth on a MuJoCo twin, each inside a tolerance fixed before the code. The nominal description fails every one of those gates. The arm probe finds the belt plane to {probe} mm, against {depth} mm from the depth camera. Arm-to-arm through the carried board is {r2r} mm off, camera-to-camera {c2c} mm. Sim only: nothing touched hardware, and the real-rig adapter waits on the belt's motor and serials.

Recovery: estimate vs hidden truth (seed 0)

Linear values in mm, angles in degrees. Error = estimate - truth (for vectors and rotations, the angle between them). Tolerance = the card's gate (sim2real.cards.conveyor_belt.TOLERANCES). Nominal = the description's own value scored the same way; it must fail, so every gate is shown able to fail.

{eval_rows}

Read the camera-2 hand-off ({c2h} mm) beside the camera-to-camera truth error ({c2c} mm). The transform fitted through the belt absorbs camera 1's focal and depth bias, and it reproduces the same bias when predicting. Board hand-offs between the cameras are therefore nearly exact in their own measured frames, even though the absolute extrinsic is {c2c} mm off. Use the truth error for anything that mixes camera geometry with the arm.

The routines

Every routine is a Step: (description, rig, context) -> context. ConveyorBelt(NOMINAL, rig).calibrate() runs them in order; .eval_twin() checks the result on held-out hand-offs. A routine reaches the rig only through the BeltRig verbs (move_belt, capture, stream, line_scan, probe, touch, place_board), so the same code runs on the twin and on the real belt.

Order to run on day one: 1 → 2 → 3 (free once 1 is done) → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8. Rung 1 is where every other rung gets its belt travel, so do it first. The real expectations are estimates: the sim has no motion blur, no lens distortion, no board warp and a kinematically perfect arm.

Runbook for the real belt (not run)

State: the Steps are written and proven in sim. The real BeltRig adapter is ~150 lines that wrap the belt drive, two RealSense cameras, arm 1's current read-back and the arms' touch. It can't be written until the belt's motor and driver are known. Until it lands, --rig real prints what it needs and exits 2 without touching anything.

Guards, every run: the belt speed is capped by --max-speed (0.2 m/s, or 0.4 m/s for the line scan only), and every move is a bounded rev count the plan prints first. Run --dry-run first, keep a hand on the e-stop, and never let the drive be autodetected (pass the /dev/serial/by-id path). The arm probe descends at 5 mm/s and stops at 6 N or 30 mm of travel, whichever comes first, with arm 1's current limit set low. Arm touches use teach-mode jogging at reduced speed.

  • Cameras: the RealSense serials for camera 1 and camera 2, and camera 1's hand-eye file.
  • Belt geometry: width, length, the belt's height under arm 1, and where camera 1, camera 2, arm 2 and the line scanner sit. These become the description's nominals; today they are a placeholder layout.
  • Equipment: the 7 x 5 ChArUco board printed on 3 mm aluminium composite (DICT_5X5_100, 40 / 30 mm), with four corner divots for the arm touch; a 6 mm ball tip for arm 1; 15 kg of sandbags for the slip run.

Decisions taken for you, caveats, not done

  • Decided: a new type module (cards/conveyor_belt.py, models/conveyor_belt.py) beside the existing actuator card registry.CONVEYOR. The existing card is the belt's dynamics; the new one is its geometry. Both are additive. Undo: git revert the two commits on claude/conveyor-type.
  • Decided: a vision extra (opencv-python-headless 4.11.0.86) for the ChArUco verbs, so the stable folders still load without OpenCV.
  • Decided: every belt carry between stations is a whole number of drum turns, which makes the drum's runout cancel exactly.
  • Decided: the probe reads contact as the zero-force intercept, not the threshold crossing, so the reading doesn't depend on belt stiffness.
  • Coordination: a peer lane is building the camera types (models/camera_rgbd.py). The twin's CameraNoise is a one-dataclass stand-in to swap for it after rebasing; I touched none of its files.
  • Caveats: the sim arm is kinematically perfect, and arm 1's own error adds directly to the probe's plane. The board is perfectly flat and the lens has no distortion. There is no motion blur in the latency or line-scan streams. Arm 2's touch is the divot's true position plus 0.3 mm of noise, not a contact simulation.
  • Not done: the real BeltRig adapter (it waits on the motor type), registering these routines into cards/calibration.py (that is the peer's write set this week), and merging the branch (it rebases onto camera-types first).
  • The paper: no paper describing an aluminium ChArUco board that calibrates a hyperspectral line scanner against a robot arm and an RGB-D camera could be confirmed. The closest confirmed match is Alexakis et al., Enhanced Waste Sorting Technology by Integrating Hyperspectral and RGB Imaging, MDPI Recycling 10(5):179 (2025): aluminium-foil ArUco markers on black card, an HSI and an RGB camera on a conveyor, registered by encoder ticks and a homography.
